Built for bad days

It assumes you'll have a day where your brain fully clocks out. Then it plans for it.

Most apps are built for your main-character best day. These are the rules Dayfall runs on your worst one.

One screen, one day.

It opens on today. Nothing else gets to fight for your attention.

One thing today.

Every day has one starred task with its own big Start button. Do that one and you're done. Fr, that's the whole bar.

An "I'm stuck" button that's always there.

Three no-brainer steps — water, sit down, smallest visible thing — plus a 10-minute timer. Zero decisions.

Missed days don't shout.

No red badges, no guilt-tripping. Once a day it shows yesterday's leftovers: keep what still matters, punt it to today or any day this week, or let it go. Every answer is valid.

No streaks, no points.

Streaks punish bad days; this app forgives them on purpose. The only thing it counts is what you did today — never what you missed.

Undo, not "are you sure?"

Fat-finger something and there's an Undo. No "are you sure??" popups standing between you and starting.

"Just the first 5."

Every big task's Start button has a smaller one under it: five minutes, nothing more. Starting is the whole boss fight, so the first step is tiny.

Time you can see.

The timer is a shrinking bar, not just digits — with a nudge at the halfway mark and at five minutes left. The screen stays awake, the tab counts down, and the app icon shows the days-left number.

Routines without retyping.

Mark a task as repeating — meds, laundry, watering the plants — and it appears every day through the deadline. Check it off or don't; tomorrow it's simply there again.

What it looks like

Quiet on purpose.

One color means time. One message means you're done. Everything else stays out of the way — and at night it follows your phone into dark mode, obviously.

For partners, roommates, and helpers

Help without having to ask what to do.

If someone you love is staring down a deadline, the worst part of "helping" is that they have to manage you too. Dayfall hands you a list of your own.

Install

It's a website that lives on your home screen.

No app store, no account, no 400MB download. Add it once and it opens full-screen like any app, works offline, and remembers your plan.

iPhone / iPad

  1. Open Dayfall in Safari.
  2. Tap Share (the square with the arrow).
  3. Tap Add to Home Screen, then Add.

Android

  1. Open Dayfall in Chrome.
  2. Tap Add on the banner, or the ⋮ menu → Install app.
  3. Find it with your other apps.
Questions

The short version.

Is this for people with an ADHD diagnosis?

It's for anyone who freezes at the start of things or loses track of how many days are left. Diagnosed or not. If "I know what to do, I just can't start" sounds familiar, it's for you.

Why fixed minute options instead of typing a number?

Because choosing between 5, 10, 15, 30, 45, 60, or 90 takes a second. Estimating "how long will this take?" from scratch is exactly the kind of decision that stops people from starting.

What happens if I miss three days?

You open the app and it shows today. That's it. Nothing turns red, nothing counts your missed days, no streak gets torched. We don't do shame here.

Where is my data?

On your phone. Sync is off unless you turn it on with your email; then your plans are stored in an account only you can read. No ads, no tracking, no selling anything about you. There's also a one-tap backup in the app's ⋯ menu — a file you keep, and an import that restores it.

Does the timer keep going if I switch apps?

Yes — it counts from the moment you tapped, so coming back always shows the right time or the "you're allowed to stop" message. Turn on alerts and it can tap you on the shoulder at zero even from another app. On iPhone, add Dayfall to your home screen first (iOS 16.4+) — that's what lets a web app send notifications at all.

What happens when the deadline arrives?

Day 0 is deadline day. After it passes, Dayfall adds up what you actually did — tasks done, minutes on timers — tells you "You made it," and offers to archive the countdown. Archived countdowns can be restored anytime from the menu.

What does it cost?

Nothing right now. If it ever does, the countdown, timers, and "I'm stuck" will stay free — those are the whole point.
